Breaking: Anthropic leaks “public” chats onto the internet.

They leaked their source code; now they "leaked" your chats.

While Anthropic is in the news warning about the risks of open-weight models, it may have a more immediate privacy problem to answer for.

Users reported that Claude shared-chat URLs were appearing in search results.
“Shared” is doing a lot of work there.
Maybe you sent a chat to a group. Maybe you wanted to save a link for later. Maybe you assumed an unguessable URL was a boundary. Then it becomes discoverable on the open web.

Some users had found chats involving someone setting up a Solana ( crypto ) , wallet, a concerning one about a lawyer in Kansas asking if they need to self-report in case of a breach of conduct violation.

I wanted to look into it and see if there was any personally identifiable data in these chats. And voila I hit the motherlode. 

I found a shared conversation where someone was using Claude to write and edit a "Security Engineering" blog post.

The chat contained their name. The project had public traces. Public professional details corroborated the author’s identity and role at a major company (Hint: lake + chips). 

I cross-verified between the blog mentioned in the chats and their LinkedIn profile and sent them a quick message. 

But this is not on them; Anthropic created the public page, and a search engine indexed it. This seems to me either a "Vibe-Coding" failure or a MAJOR OVERSIGHT. 

I use Claude to think out loud. They paste in career plans, side projects, code, finances, work context, and drafts they would never publish under their own name.

If you build AI for that kind of trust, privacy cannot depend on users reverse-engineering what “Share” means after their conversations start appearing in search.

We taught rocks to think. That doesn’t mean we get to stop.
