Spent Friday evening at NUS Hackers' 300th Friday Hacks, and it was a pretty great way to cap off the week 
 
The first talk, by Ron Tay from Featherless AI was on The Long Tail of AI, particularly why the future of AI probably isn't one enormous model doing everything, but a much larger ecosystem of specialised models and multi-model systems.

What stood out to me was understanding the reasoning behind the trends: lower latency at the edge, better performance on narrow domains, more personalised systems, and less dependence on a single model provider. 
  
One thing I particularly enjoyed was getting to pick Ron's brain afterwards. We ended up talking about things like whether the growth in demand for AI inference can continue to outpace the efficiency gains we're seeing in hardware and models, and what that means for the economics of serving increasingly specialised models.

Timothy Ye also gave a very different kind of talk on maintaining GoDNS for more than a decade. Less about the glamorous side of open source, and more about the surprisingly difficult parts: saying no to features, designing something strangers can contribute to, and keeping a project alive without burning yourself out.
 
And because this was Friday Hacks #300, we obviously had to bring out Quack and sing happy birthday. 🐥
 
Good talks, interesting conversations, some merch, and a giant duck. Hard to complain about that Friday night.
 
Thanks for putting this  together NUS Hackers — here's to the next 300!
