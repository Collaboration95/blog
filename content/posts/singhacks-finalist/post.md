At 6pm on Saturday, we submitted our SingHacks 2026 project and gave a
presentation to the judges.

The finalist announcement was at 8pm.

I had 2 hours to kill. So I opened up my own coding-agent conversation history.

Hackathons compress an unusual amount of thinking into a small room and are a melting pot of ideas.

One idea that stuck with me was from Joel Mun, Ripple’s feedback hook in Codex. When an agent hit friction with XRPL docs, it would be
captured as feedback and sent to a Cloudflare D1 database.

I realised that my own agent sessions contained a similar kind of signal.

They were not just prompts and answers. They contained:

- what I thought the problem was at each stage;
- which ideas I rejected, and why;
- the moments where i had a good idea but did not fit the hackathon

I decided to take a look, and after an hour of cleaning up JSON records, deleting unnecessary fields, and grouping the sessions and messages by intent, the hackathon looked different in retrospect.

The final product did not arrive as one brilliant idea on the first try.

I started by asking what XRPL, x402, and the starter kit hinted at.
Then explored agent spending controls, buying evidence before paying a
suspicious invoice, media licensing, and other ways an agent could discover and purchase a useful digital service.

The ideas improved when we gave them a stricter test:

Does the problem exist without blockchain?
What exact value appears after the payment?
What should be blocked, and can a reviewer understand what happened later?

That process led us to ResearchAgent: a budget-aware research desk where an
agent decides what paywalled articles are worth paying for, then returns a deeply cited, reviewable answer.

The  lesson is that an LLM conversation can be a record of intent,
uncertainty, and decision-making—if you treat it as an artefact worth examining,
rather than something to discard once the code works.

At 8 pm, I got the result: The Fast and the Fungible were finalists.

Our team will be presenting ResearchAgent at the Singapore FinTech Festival at the Swiss Podium. 

I intend to spend them in much the same way I spent those two hours: keeping the decision trail, testing our assumptions, and sharpening the product rather than simply adding more to it.
